## Response

## Five editing-reward dimensions

The paper evaluates editing along five dimensions:

1. **Instruction fulfillment** — whether the requested changes are semantically correct and complete.
2. **Edit execution quality** — the visual quality and natural integration of the edited target region.
3. **Overall visual quality** — global technical quality and stylistic coherence.
4. **Text-editing quality** — textual correctness and rendering quality, when text is modified.
5. **Unedited-region preservation** — whether unrelated regions remain semantically, structurally, and visually unchanged. [1] [2]

## Combination into one scalar reward

For criteria with multiple sub-dimensions, the minimum sub-score is retained. After normalization, the applicable dimension scores are combined using a **minimum, or bottleneck, operation**:

\[
R_{\mathrm{edit}}
=
\min\left(
\{R_{\mathrm{inst}},R_{\mathrm{exec}},R_{\mathrm{visual}},R_{\mathrm{pres}}\}
\cup
\{R_{\mathrm{text}}\mid \text{text edit is applicable}\}
\right).
\]

Thus, the weakest applicable dimension determines the final scalar reward; the scores are not averaged or summed. The text-editing score is omitted when no text modification is requested, and the edit-execution score is set to zero if the requested change is not visibly realized. [1]

**Ambiguity/limitation:** the provided excerpt states that the scores are normalized, but it does not specify the normalization formula or its range. Also, the paper separately describes OCR and HPSv3++ reward routing for other prompt partitions; those scores are not summed or normalized with each other, which should not be confused with the editing-reward aggregation above.
