## Response

## Core difference

SenseNova-U1.5 keeps the same basic type of conditioning: it represents the noise scale associated with the target resolution, normalizes it, encodes it with a dedicated sinusoidal MLP, and adds that embedding to the diffusion-timestep representation:

\[
\mathbf{s}_t=\bm{\tau}_t+\mathrm{NSEmb}(\bar{\sigma}_R).
\]

Thus, the denoiser receives both diffusion-time information and resolution-dependent noise information. [1]

The extension compared with SenseNova-U1 is the **calibration range of the normalization**:

- **SenseNova-U1:** the normalization range was calibrated up to \(2048\times 2048\).
- **SenseNova-U1.5:** the reference resolution for normalization, and hence \(\sigma_{\max}\), was extended to \(4096\times4096\). [2]

Formally, U1.5 defines

\[
\bar{\sigma}_{R}
=
\frac{\sigma_R(H,W)}{\sigma_{\max}},
\]

where \(\sigma_{\max}\) corresponds to the \(4096\times4096\) reference resolution. [1]

## Interpretation

Therefore, the change is not presented as a new kind of conditioning signal. Rather, U1.5 extends the **resolution range over which the noise-scale conditioning is normalized and calibrated**, allowing the model to account for native high-resolution generation up to \(4096\times4096\).
