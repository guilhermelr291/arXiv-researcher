## Effect of CoT on SenseNova-U1.5

In RISEBench, **CoT means prompting the model to reason before producing the edited image**.[1] Comparing the two SenseNova-U1.5 settings gives the following results:[1]

| Edit type | Without CoT | With CoT | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Temporal | 34.1 | 38.8 | **+4.7** |
| Causal | 37.8 | 52.2 | **+14.4** |
| Spatial | 49.0 | 42.0 | **−7.0** |
| Logical | 10.6 | 20.0 | **+9.4** |
| **Overall** | **33.6** | **38.6** | **+5.0** |

The overall score therefore rises from **33.6 to 38.6**, a 5.0-point improvement with CoT.[1] The paper also states that SenseNova-U1.5 already outperforms the evaluated open-source baselines without CoT, while explicit reasoning produces a further substantial improvement.[2]

### Which edit types benefit most and least?

- **Most benefit: causal edits.** Their score increases by **14.4 points**, from 37.8 to 52.2.[1]
- **Second-largest benefit: logical edits.** Their score increases by **9.4 points**, from 10.6 to 20.0.[1]
- **Temporal edits** also improve, by **4.7 points**.[1]
- **Least benefit: spatial edits.** Their score decreases by **7.0 points**, from 49.0 to 42.0, so CoT does not consistently help this category in the reported comparison.[1]

The authors interpret the larger gains in causal, logical, and temporal editing as evidence that CoT is especially useful when the required transformation must be inferred rather than directly localized.[2] They describe spatial editing as benefiting less consistently and suggest that explicit reasoning is more valuable for implicit, multi-step transformations than for directly grounded edits.[2]

### Limitations and apparent tension

The qualitative discussion says that gains are most pronounced for **causal, logical, and temporal** edits, while spatial editing benefits less consistently.[2] The exact table comparison is more specific: causal editing benefits most, but spatial editing actually declines by 7.0 points.[1] Thus, the qualitative statement should not be read as saying that every edit type improves with CoT.

The provided evidence reports scores for one CoT and one non-CoT configuration, but it does not provide variance estimates, significance tests, or an analysis of why spatial performance falls.[1] Therefore, the results support an association between CoT and higher overall performance—especially on causal and logical edits—but do not by themselves establish that CoT is universally beneficial or explain all category-level changes.
